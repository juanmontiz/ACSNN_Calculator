# Z_P² scaling analysis of the CDW-EIS cross sections

Generated by `MODEL_SET=b100k python3 zp2_analysis.py`. Target H, theory **CDW-EIS only** (37 energies per charge,
10–10⁵ keV/u, Z_P = 1–9). Metrics use the valid points (E ≥ 50 keV/u, 33 per charge).

Models:
- **ACSNN interpolator**: the network of the ACSNN calculator, trained on all charges and theories
  (CDW-EIS, CTMC, Rudd), copied to `models_folder/acsnn_interpolator_weights.npz`. Its in-sample error on CDW-EIS is
  MAE = 0.0085 in log10 σ.
- **Hole models**: the 129 networks `best_statedict_gauss_128_hole_XYZ_b100k.pth` (CDW-EIS + CTMC, one, two or three
  charges removed), evaluated on their excluded charges (333 cases).

## Quantities

In first Born approximation, at fixed projectile velocity, the ionization cross section scales as σ(Z_P, E) = Z_P² σ_p(E).
Three quantities measure the deviation from that law:

| Quantity | Definition | First-Born value |
|---|---|---|
| Scaled cross section | σ / Z_P² | independent of Z_P |
| Scaling ratio | R(Z_P, E) = σ(Z_P, E) / [Z_P² σ_CDW-EIS(1, E)] | 1 |
| Effective charge exponent | n_eff(Z_P, E) = ∂ ln σ / ∂ ln Z_P | 2 |

- For the CDW-EIS data, n_eff is obtained from finite differences on the integer charges: centred,
  [ln σ(Z+1) − ln σ(Z−1)] / [ln(Z+1) − ln(Z−1)], for Z = 2–8, and one-sided at Z = 1 and Z = 9.
- For the networks, Z is a continuous input, so n_eff = Z ln(10) ∂ŷ/∂Z is computed **exactly** by automatic
  differentiation. These are the lines in the figures. For the metrics, the networks are compared with the reference
  on the **same finite-difference stencil**, because a one-sided difference at Z = 1 or Z = 9 is not comparable with
  a local derivative.

### Physics baselines for the hole experiments

To judge whether the network predictions of the excluded charges are good, they are compared with three simple
predictions. Each one uses only the CDW-EIS values of the charges the hole model was trained on:

| Baseline | Prediction for an excluded charge z |
|---|---|
| **Born** | z² σ(1, E), the first-Born scaling of the proton cross section (only when Z = 1 is a training charge, 232 cases) |
| **Z² from nearest** | (z/t)² σ(t, E), with t the nearest training charge. If two training charges are equally close, the two estimates are averaged in log10; for an isolated hole this amounts to an interpolation between z − 1 and z + 1 with a Z² curvature correction |
| **Power law in Z** | σ ∝ Z^n through two training charges: the nearest below and above z (interpolation), or the two nearest on one side (extrapolation) |

## 1. CDW-EIS reference: how far from Z_P² scaling?

Median R and n_eff of the CDW-EIS data by energy band (`reference_scaling_by_band.csv`):

| Z_P | R, 50–500 keV/u | R, 500–5000 | R, 5000–10⁵ | n_eff, 50–500 | n_eff, 500–5000 | n_eff, 5000–10⁵ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | 0.71 | 0.98 | 0.996 | 1.36 | 1.95 | 1.99 |
| 3 | 0.49 | 0.95 | 0.991 | 0.99 | 1.89 | 1.99 |
| 5 | 0.26 | 0.86 | 0.980 | 0.46 | 1.76 | 1.97 |
| 7 | 0.14 | 0.78 | 0.968 | 0.03 | 1.66 | 1.96 |
| 9 | 0.08 | 0.71 | 0.957 | −0.26 | 1.60 | 1.95 |

- **High energy.** CDW-EIS approaches first-Born scaling. Above 5 MeV/u, R lies within 4 % of 1 and n_eff = 1.95–1.99
  for every charge. Even at 10⁵ keV/u the deviation grows with Z_P (R = 0.957 for Z_P = 9).
- **Intermediate and low energy.** The scaling breaks down. Below 500 keV/u the cross section grows much more slowly
  than Z_P²: R drops to 0.08 for Z_P = 9.
- **Saturation.** For Z_P ≥ 8 between 50 and 500 keV/u the exponent becomes negative: σ *decreases* with Z_P.
- **Consequence for the NN.** The charge dependence the network has to learn changes from n_eff = 2 at high energy to
  n_eff < 0 at low energy, and the transition moves to higher energy as Z_P grows.

- **The interpolator reproduces the Z_P² scaling and its breakdown.**
  - R(Z_P, E) is reproduced within 1–2 % for all charges and energies (`acsnn_scaled_cross_section.png`).
  - On the integer charges the charge exponent agrees with the finite-difference reference within 0.01–0.07.
- **Between and at the edges of the training charges the exact derivative is not fully physical**
  (`acsnn_effective_exponent.png`):
  - At high energy n_eff tends to 1.74 at Z_P = 1 and 1.87 at Z_P = 9 instead of 2.
  - A region with n_eff > 2.1 appears between Z_P ≈ 1.3 and 2 at 300–10⁴ keV/u.
  - The network fits the values at the integer charges, but nothing constrains the slope in Z away from them. Predictions
    for non-integer Z_P, or the use of its derivative, should be treated with caution near Z_P = 1–2 and Z_P = 9.

## 3. Hole models

### Error by case class (median per-case MAE in log10 σ, valid points)

| Case class | Cases | NN | Born | Z² from nearest | Power law | NN better than Born / Z² / power |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| interp. gap 1 | 154 | 0.011 | 0.177 | 0.0010 | 0.0036 | 89 % / 0 % / 2 % |
| interp. gap 2 | 72 | 0.016 | 0.177 | 0.043 | 0.0073 | 90 % / 88 % / 8 % |
| interp. gap 3 | 15 | 0.026 | 0.177 | 0.041 | 0.013 | 93 % / 53 % / 7 % |
| extrap. d = 1 | 74 | 0.060 | 0.339 | 0.042 | 0.0096 | 100 % / 34 % / 0 % |
| extrap. d = 2 | 16 | 0.349 | 0.339 | 0.085 | 0.030 | 100 % / 19 % / 0 % |
| extrap. d = 3 | 2 | 1.20 | 0.339 | 0.126 | 0.081 | – / 0 % / 0 % |

All differences between the NN and the Z²-from-nearest and power-law baselines are significant (Wilcoxon p < 0.003),
except NN vs Z² from nearest for gap 3 (p = 0.60). Full tables: `holes_by_case_class.csv`,
`holes_by_n_excluded_and_case_class.csv`, `holes_by_charge.csv`, `holes_cases.csv`.

1. **The hole models capture the deviation from Z_P² scaling much better than first-Born scaling.** They beat the pure Born
   prediction z²σ_p in 91 % of the cases where it can be built. Born scaling fails below ~1 MeV/u (MAE ≈ 0.18–0.34).
2. **Local scaling from the neighbouring training charges is far more accurate than the network.**
   - A power law in Z through the two neighbouring charges reproduces the excluded charge 3–6 × better than the NN in
     interpolation (0.0036 vs 0.011 for isolated holes), and the NN beats it in only 3 % of all cases.
   - In extrapolation the gap widens: d = 1 gives 0.0096 vs 0.060, and d = 2 gives 0.030 vs 0.35.
   - Z² scaling from the nearest charge is even better for isolated holes (0.001), but worse than the NN for wider gaps.
   - The CDW-EIS cross section is so smooth in Z_P that interpolating it in ln Z is almost exact. The network, which is
     fitted to CDW-EIS and CTMC together with a single set of weights and has an in-sample error of ≈ 0.01, does not
     reach that accuracy.
3. **The error of the NN is an error in the charge exponent.** Its exponent error at the excluded charge (same stencil)
   correlates with its cross-section error (Spearman ρ = 0.58, p = 10⁻³¹), and grows from gap 1 (median 0.046) to gap 3
   (0.19), d = 1 (0.86), d = 2 (2.1) and d = 3 (4.2) (`holes_exponent_error.png`).

### Behaviour at the edges (`holes_scaling_ratio.png`, `holes_effective_exponent.png`)

- **Z_P = 1, extrapolation.**
  - The hole models predict R ≈ 2–3 instead of 1, at all energies.
  - Their exact charge exponent at Z_P = 1 is close to zero (between −1 and +0.8), where CDW-EIS gives 1.5–2: the
    networks continue the cross section toward low Z_P almost flat instead of with the Z_P² law. On the stencil between
    Z_P = 1 and 2 the exponent is underestimated by 1.1 (d = 1), 2.5 (d = 2) and 5.1 (d = 3).
  - This is the mechanism behind the factor ~2 overestimation of σ(Z_P = 1).
  - Born-like scaling from Z_P = 2 or a power law from Z_P = 2 and 3 would predict Z_P = 1 with MAE 0.036–0.043,
    versus 0.33 for the NN at d = 1.
- **Z_P = 2, interpolation.**
  - The finite-difference exponent across Z_P = 1–3 is correct (bias −0.007), but the value at Z_P = 2 is too low
    (MBE −0.067, MAE 0.067): the predicted σ(Z_P) has the wrong curvature between the training charges 1 and 3.
  - The exact exponent at Z_P = 2 rises to 2.2–2.6 above 10³ keV/u, and R drifts down to 0.7–0.9.
- **Z_P = 9, extrapolation.**
  - The exponent overshoots above ~2 MeV/u (n_eff 2.5–4 instead of 2), so R rises above 1 at high energy
    (exponent bias +0.32 for d = 1, +1.0 for d = 2).
  - The cross-section error stays moderate (MAE 0.026 for d = 1) because the overshoot occurs over a short charge
    interval.

## 4. Conclusions

1. CDW-EIS follows Z_P² scaling only above a few MeV/u. At lower energies the effective exponent falls below 2 and
   becomes negative for Z_P ≥ 8, a strong departure from first-Born behaviour that any model of σ(Z_P) has to reproduce.
2. The ACSNN interpolator reproduces this behaviour on the training charges (σ within 1–2 %, exponent within 0.07),
   but its derivative with respect to Z_P is not physical near Z_P = 1–2 and at Z_P = 9.
3. When charges are removed, the networks keep the qualitative non-Born behaviour but get the charge exponent wrong:
   moderately inside the training range, strongly outside it. At Z_P = 1 they extrapolate with n_eff ≈ 0 instead of ≈ 2.
4. Simple scaling in ln Z_P from the neighbouring CDW-EIS charges predicts a missing CDW-EIS charge 3–10 × more accurately
   than the networks. Building the Z_P² scaling into the model could improve both interpolation and, above all,
   extrapolation. Two possible ways: train on σ/Z_P² (or on R), or penalize deviations of n_eff from the CDW-EIS trend.
