# `_b100k` models vs original models: the effect of removing Rudd

Generated by `MODEL_SET=b100k python3 compare_model_sets.py`; in the tables and figures the `_b100k` set is labelled `b`.

| | Original | `_b` | `_b100k` |
|---|---|---|---|
| Training data (H target) | CDW-EIS + CTMC + Rudd | CDW-EIS + CTMC | CDW-EIS + CTMC |
| Models | 165 (aab/abb duplicates) | 129 unique holes | 129 unique holes |
| Training | `train_optimum`, 100 000 epochs | sequential, ≈ 1.5 min per model (fewer epochs) | `train_ensemble`, 100 000 epochs |
| In-sample MAE log10 (median) | 0.0141 | 0.0246 | 0.0146 |

`_b100k` reaches the same in-sample fit as the original set, which confirms that the `_b` models were under-trained: from
`_b` to `_b100k` the per-case MAE drops by a median factor of 0.64–0.71 for 1, 2 and 3 excluded charges (75–78 % of cases
better, p < 1e-10), for every charge except Z = 1. The comparison below is therefore the fair test of removing Rudd.

## 1. Run-to-run noise: a built-in control

Rudd only exists for Z = 1. **Every model that excludes Z = 1 is trained on exactly the same data in both sets**, so for those
101 cases the only difference is the training randomness (unseeded 75/25 split, shuffling, dropout). They give the noise level
of a single training run:

| Cases | n | Median ratio `_b100k` / original | IQR of the ratio | Wilcoxon p |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Z = 1 excluded (identical data): control | 101 | 0.96 | 0.81–1.20 | 0.43 |
| Z = 1 in training (Rudd removed) | 232 | 0.92 | 0.67–1.29 | 0.003 |
| … of which evaluated Z = 3–8 | 174 | 0.95 | 0.72–1.32 | 0.08 |
| … of which evaluated Z = 2 | 29 | **0.74** | | **9e-7** |

A single model differs from another run on the same data by about ±20 % in MAE (IQR 0.81–1.20). Differences between two
individual models smaller than a factor of ~1.3 are not meaningful.

## 5. Conclusions

1. **Removing Rudd does not hurt.** With the same training length, interior charges (3–8) are statistically indistinguishable
   from the original set (median ratio 0.98, p = 0.20), and the in-sample fit is the same.
2. **Removing Rudd helps at Z = 2**, the charge next to the only one that had Rudd data: MAE −25 % (p = 4e-4), bias
   −0.060 → −0.047, and the single-charge model `222` goes from 23 % to 4.7 % median error. The original set had to reconcile
   Rudd with CDW-EIS/CTMC at Z = 1, which distorted the interpolation between Z = 1 and Z = 3.
3. **It also helps in wide gaps**: adjacent pairs and three-charge gaps improve by 10–25 % (p ≈ 0.01–0.05), mostly through
   cases that involve the low-Z region.
4. **Extrapolation to Z = 1 is unchanged**: those models see the same data in both sets. It remains the dominant failure
   (σ overestimated ×2 at d = 1), so it has to be addressed in another way (e.g. a physically motivated charge scaling or
   keeping Z = 1 always in training).
5. **The `_b` results were an under-training artefact**: the conclusions of `eval_holes_b/comparison/REPORT.md` about
   interior charges being worse without Rudd do not hold.
